Beyond Checklist Compliance: Risk-Based Project Quality Management

1 | The problem with isolated risk assessments

Quality documents are often prepared independently. Procurement may assess supplier risk, construction may rate site work, and commissioning may classify energization risk without a shared basis. This can produce inconsistent inspection priorities, duplicated controls, or unrecognized risks transferred between project phases.

A risk-based ITP cannot be credible merely because a column labels activities Low, Medium or High. Those ratings must be traceable to a controlled, approved project assessment that considers the whole lifecycle.

2 | Start with the Project Risk Management Procedure

Before preparing risk-based QA/QC documents, the project should establish a Project Risk Management Procedure approved by the appropriate project authorities. It defines the scope, activity breakdown, risk identification methods, likelihood and consequence scales, scoring formula, risk acceptance criteria, risk ownership, escalation thresholds, review frequency and change control.

The procedure must specify how safety, environment, technical integrity, quality, cost, schedule, operability and contractual consequences are considered. It must also state who can approve residual risks and what actions are required when a threshold is exceeded.

The procedure is the rulebook; the Project Activity Risk Assessment Matrix is the structured application of those rules.

3 | Build the comprehensive activity risk matrix

The project should maintain a controlled table covering procurement, vendor engineering, FAT, logistics, preservation, fabrication, installation, testing, pre-commissioning, commissioning, completion and handover. Every activity should have an activity/WBS reference, risk ID, hazard or failure mode, cause, consequence, likelihood, score, existing controls, proposed actions, owner, residual risk and review status.

Risk entries should be linked to the central Project Risk Register, rather than becoming an independent and contradictory QA/QC register. Detailed discipline assessments may exist, but material changes must be reconciled centrally.

4 | Risk scoring and interpretation

An illustrative 5 by 5 model calculates Risk Score = Likelihood (1–5) × Consequence (1–5). Example bands are Low 1–7, Medium 8–14 and High 15–25. These thresholds are examples only: actual bands, consequence definitions and acceptance authorities must be set by the approved project procedure.

Inherent risk is assessed before additional treatment; residual risk is reassessed after controls. Numerical scores help prioritize attention, but must not replace engineering judgment or override mandatory requirements.

5 | Carry risks through the full project lifecycle

Procurement: evaluate vendor capability, specification compliance, critical material traceability, FAT scope and delivery exposure. Fabrication and construction: assess interface clashes, workmanship, lifting/handling, preservation, installation errors and workfront readiness.

Pre-commissioning: evaluate incomplete terminations, calibration defects, protection settings, test prerequisites and outstanding punch items. Commissioning: assess energization boundaries, interlocks, system integration and operational readiness. Handover: evaluate incomplete records, unresolved critical items, as-built discrepancies and maintenance access.

Risks do not disappear at phase boundaries. A procurement deviation can become an installation defect and then a commissioning constraint if the treatment is not verified.

6 | Translate approved risk controls into PQP, procedures, ITP and ITR

Project Quality Plan (PQP): establish risk governance, roles, document interfaces, monitoring and escalation. Work procedures: translate risks into safe work sequence, prerequisites, competency, tools, preservation and contingency controls.

Inspection and Test Plans (ITPs): define inspection stages, verification techniques, witness/surveillance needs and proposed intervention intensity. Inspection and Test Records (ITRs): capture objective evidence, actual results, acceptance decisions and traceability to applicable risk controls.

The risk score informs additional controls; it does not permit deletion of contractual, regulatory or client-required hold points.

7 | Worked example: 13.8 kV switchgear

A project identifies potential damage during transport, incorrect installation, insulation defects, protection malfunction and unsafe energization. The central assessment assigns risk owners and measures, then maps them to procurement specifications, vendor FAT, storage inspection, installation procedures, pre-commissioning tests and energization authorization.

For example, a high-risk protection malfunction entry may drive approved relay-setting verification, secondary injection, trip-circuit and interlock checks, and witnessed functional testing. ITRs must record the results and link to the relevant acceptance criteria.

8 | Review, feedback and governance

Trigger risk reassessment after design revisions, vendor deviations, nonconformities, repeated inspection failures, new site interfaces, schedule compression, test failures and commissioning findings. The central register should show owners, deadlines, action status and approval history.

Quality metrics may include first-pass acceptance, repeat NCRs, overdue high-risk actions, inspection readiness, change cycle time, failed tests and unresolved critical handover items. Metrics should inform action, not simply populate dashboards.

9 | Decision principle and conclusion

Risk-based quality management is not a method for reducing inspection paperwork at all costs. It is a disciplined method for placing prevention and verification where failure matters most, while maintaining all non-negotiable requirements.

Prepare the risk procedure first. Assess the complete activity lifecycle second. Approve and control the central risk baseline third. Only then translate the risk treatments into PQP, work procedures, ITPs and ITRs, and continuously feed results back into the project risk system.
